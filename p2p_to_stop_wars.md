## We're not talking about gaming and piracy

All of modern conflict relies on one trick: that the person 2000km away is an abstraction you never talk to directly. That all your messages to them go through an organization that gets to edit, block, or re-label who they are.

"Drive safer. Don't pollute. Watch your words, you might hurt someone." We're taught that our actions land on real people - which is true when you share a road, or air, or a wire.

Then we're told the opposite: fund this, build this weapon, block those people from your app store. Suddenly caring about a stranger you've never met is a duty *to injure* them, not to talk to them.

Both messages are the same: "Let us decide which others you count."

P2P breaks that trick at the lowest level. Two laptops, one UDP packet with `[{"ChatMessage":{"message":"hi"}}]` - no relay that can later say "you're not allowed to say hi to that IP."

Does that end wars tomorrow? No. Obviously we're missing pieces.

But without a plain wire, none of those higher pieces can even exist censorship-resistant. With it, they can.

So this isn't a chat app. It's the desert the chat apps can be built on. A legible, low-latency, future-proof commons where packets are directly from people, not from organizations.

If you appreciate low latency, legibility, and future-proofed interoperability - this is for you. Let's share peers even if we don't share apps.

Let's make it hard for anyone to tell us who we're allowed to talk to before we all kill each other.
